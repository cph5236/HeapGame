Accessibility

Last reviewed: November 2026

Heap is a free game made by a small team. We want as many people as possible to be able to use this site and play the game, and we treat accessibility problems as bugs.

Our goal

We aim for the website and its menus around the game to conform to the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1 at level AA.

The game itself is a real-time, visual platformer. Under WCAG, content whose purpose is a specific sensory experience is only required to offer a descriptive text alternative, and the game canvas has one. We do not claim that real-time play works with a screen reader. We do provide the accommodations below.

In the game

  • Keyboard play. Move with the arrow keys or A/D, jump with ↑ or W, dash with Shift, dive with ↓ or S, place with Space and grab with E. On touch screens you can choose tilt or an on-screen joystick, on either side.
  • No keyboard trap. Tab and Shift+Tab always move focus out of the game, and the game never captures keys while you are using a link or control on the page.
  • Pause. Esc or P pauses a run. A run also pauses itself when you switch tabs, when the window loses focus, or when you move focus out of the game. (The short opening tutorial has no pause yet.)
  • Reduce flashing & motion. No brightness flash in the game repeats more than three times per second. The Reduce Flashing setting (Settings → Controls) also turns off screen shake, full-screen flashes, confetti and particle trails, and stills or slows the remaining effects. Its default, Auto, follows your device's reduced-motion setting; choose On or Off to override it.
  • Not colour alone. Off-screen markers use a different shape as well as a different colour for enemies (a notched arrow) and salvage (a round pin).
  • Announcements. Pausing, resuming and the result of a run are announced to screen readers.
  • Separate volume controls. Music, player, enemy and environment sounds each have their own volume setting.

Known limitations

  • The game's menus (main menu, settings, pause, store, results) are drawn inside the game canvas, so a screen reader cannot read or operate them, and some cannot be reached with the keyboard alone. We plan to move them to standard HTML controls.
  • Controls cannot yet be remapped, and there is no separate text-size setting inside the game. Browser zoom enlarges the page around the game.
  • On narrow screens the "How it works" panels beside the game are hidden to make room for it; the same summary is available to assistive technology through the game's description.

Tell us about a problem

If something on this site or in the game is hard or impossible for you to use, please tell us — what you were trying to do, and the device, browser and any assistive technology you use. Email HanlinsoftwareSWS@gmail.com, or use Feedback in the game's main menu. We aim to reply within a week.

This site has no accessibility overlay widgets. Fixes are made in the site and game themselves.
